How to do a takeoff from a PDF plan on an iPad

Open the plan set PDF in a takeoff app, set the scale of each sheet before measuring anything on it, then go trade by trade: count what is counted, trace what is measured in feet, outline what is measured in square feet. Check one known dimension on every sheet.

  1. Get the PDF, not a picture of it. A plan set saved from CAD has lines an app can snap to. A scan or a photo can still be measured, but taps land where you put them.
  2. Import it and pick an item list for your trade, so you are not typing item names on a ladder.
  3. Set the scale of each sheet. Use the scale printed on the sheet, or calibrate from a dimension you trust. Details and enlarged plans on the same sheet often have their own scale.
  4. Check it. Measure a second printed dimension. If it is off, the sheet was resized: calibrate from the dimension instead.
  5. Count symbols one item at a time across the sheet, so a missed corner shows.
  6. Measure runs (conduit, pipe, base, wall) by tapping along them, and areas (floor, slab, roof) by their corners. Cut out openings.
  7. Split by floor or area if you will be asked for it; it is far quicker now than later.
  8. Price and export. Add unit costs and waste, then overhead, profit and tax, and send the quantities to Excel or as a marked-up plan.

Questions

Can I do a takeoff on an iPhone?

Yes. Counting works well on a phone; tracing long runs is easier on an iPad or a Mac.

Do I need an internet connection?

Not with Tally: the plan and the takeoff are on the device.

What if the plan is a scan?

Calibrate from a printed dimension, and check a second one near the opposite corner: scans can be stretched.
