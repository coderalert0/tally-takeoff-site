How to do a drywall takeoff from a floor plan

Wall area = length of wall × height × the number of sides that get board. Subtract large openings, add ceilings, add waste, and divide by the area of the sheet you hang: 32 square feet for 4×8, 48 for 4×12.

Steps

  1. Read the wall types legend first. A partition with one layer each side and one with two layers on one side are different items.
  2. Trace each wall type as its own run, at the sheet's scale.
  3. Multiply by height. Board to deck and board to ceiling are different heights on the same plan; the sections say which.
  4. Multiply by sides, and by layers where the type calls for two.
  5. Take doors and windows off if you price labor by area; many leave small openings in because the cut-outs are waste.
  6. Outline ceilings by type from the reflected ceiling plan, not the floor plan.

Worked example

180 ft of partition, 9 ft high, one layer both sides: 180 × 9 × 2 = 3,240 sq ft. Take off six 3 × 7 ft doors on both sides: 6 × 21 × 2 = 252 sq ft, leaving 2,988. With 10% for waste that is 3,287 sq ft, or 69 sheets of 4 × 12.

In Tally

Give a run a wall height and it totals wall area as well as length; waste is per item. Starter lists include drywall and framing, and a count of sheets or studs can hang off each item as an assembly.

Questions

How many square feet is a 4×12 sheet?

48.

Do I subtract doors and windows?

It depends on how you price. Taking out large openings is usual; leaving small ones in covers cutting waste. Do the same on every job.

Where do ceiling areas come from?

The reflected ceiling plan, which shows each ceiling type's extent.
