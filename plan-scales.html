Architectural and engineering plan scales, and how to set them

A scale says how much real length one inch of the sheet stands for. 1/4" = 1'-0" means one inch is 4 feet, a ratio of 1:48. 1" = 20' means one inch is 20 feet, 1:240. If the sheet was printed or saved at another size, the printed scale is wrong: calibrate from a dimension.

Half-size sets

An ARCH D sheet (36 by 24 in) printed on 17 by 11 in paper is a little under half size. On an exact half-size print, 1/4" = 1'-0" measures as 1/8" = 1'-0", so a length taken at the printed scale comes out half what it is, and an area a quarter. Look for a graphic scale bar: it shrinks with the sheet, so it stays true.

How to check a scale

  1. Find the longest dimension string on the sheet.
  2. Measure it at the scale you set.
  3. Off by a fixed factor: the sheet was resized, so calibrate from that dimension.
  4. Right across and wrong up and down: the scan is stretched. Measure in each direction from a dimension in that direction.

Questions

What ratio is 1/8" = 1'-0"?

1:96. One inch on the sheet is 8 feet.

What does NTS mean?

Not to scale: do not measure it. Use the dimensions written on it.

Why are my areas about a quarter of what they should be?

The sheet is at half size and measured at its full-size scale. Lengths come out half, so areas come out a quarter.
